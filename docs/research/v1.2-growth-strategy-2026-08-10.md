## BOTTOM LINE

TruNorth does not have a marketing problem yet. **It has a correctness problem that gets worse with
every visitor.** I verified, in the repo, four defects that are live right now:

| Defect | Verified count | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Pages asserting an **"F" grade on companies never graded** | **9,765** | `api/alternatives-seo.js:63-64` — `Number(null)`→`0`→`isFinite`→`grade(0)`="F" |
| `"Claude AI synthesis"` cited as a **source** on a "records, not opinions" product | **82,553 strings across 11,187 brand files** | `public/data/companies/*.json` |
| Graded brands resting on a **single category** | **1,298 (42%)** — of which **586 score exactly 50.000, which renders as a "B"** | `csc` in per-brand files |
| Provably false public claim | Home Depot **"pay ratio 2026:1, CEO total comp $2K, DEF 14A 2011"** — the parser read the *year* as the ratio | `home-depot.json` |

Concrete consequence: **23andMe currently grades "B"** — from one `privacy: "mixed"` datapoint — while
its own narrative on the same record says the California AG is suing it over a data breach.

**The biggest risk to a big-bang launch is not that it fails. It is that it works.** Every one of the
above detonates precisely when attention arrives, and credibility is spent once. All of them are
days of work, not months, and none needs a decision from Aron.

**Recommendation: Fix → Fill → Launch, over ~6 weeks. Do not fire the launch in August.**
The launch is real and worth building toward — but it should go out in late September against a
product that survives a journalist checking it.

## 2. THE SINGLE BIGGEST PRODUCT WIN AVAILABLE

**The search box cannot find the products people actually buy — even though the answer already exists
in the repo.**

Verified independently:
- The scanner loads `brand-parent-map.json` (`App.jsx:149-190`); **the text-search path never does**
  (`App.jsx:5483-5521`, MiniSearch over `search-index.json`).
- **4,902 sub-brand names that resolve to an ALREADY-GRADED parent are absent from search.**
  4,389 of them are `confidence: "high"`.
- Concentrated exactly where people shop: Nestlé 206 · P&G 180 · Unilever 179 · PepsiCo 124 ·
  Newell 118 · Diageo 116 · Mars 115 · Keurig Dr Pepper 114 · General Mills 101 · Hershey 96 · Coca-Cola 95.
- The map is *correct and already written*: `tide → procter-and-gamble (high)`, `oreo → mondelez (high)`,
  `cheerios → general-mills (high)`.

The absurdity, proven: **Febreze, Downy, Gillette, Olay, Pantene, Head & Shoulders all resolve and grade
C. Tide, Charmin, Pampers, Old Spice, Swiffer — same parent — return nothing at all.**

Shopper-basket test (99 real everyday brands): 67% graded, 3 tracked-but-ungraded, **30 absent entirely**
— and ~26 of those 30 have a graded parent sitting in the catalog right now.

### The right design (not just "add 4,902 rows")
Do **not** materialize 4,902 new catalog rows — that inflates the brand count dishonestly and multiplies
the duplicate-grade problem below. Instead:
- **One record, many aliases.** "Tide" resolves to Procter & Gamble's record.
- **Label it honestly:** *"Tide — made by Procter & Gamble. Graded C on the parent company."*
- Never let an alias overwrite a brand that has its own real record (Ben & Jerry's is independently A;
  Unilever is C — that distinction is a feature, keep it).

### Related credibility bug to fix in the same pass
Same company, contradictory grades, live today:
`Alaska Air Group=D` vs `Alaska Airlines=A` · `Apple=B` vs `Apple Store=A` vs `Apple Music=C` ·
`Amazon=C` vs `Amazon Go=F` · `CVS Health=D` vs `CVS Pharmacy=C` vs `CVS Pharmacy y más=B`.
Measured: of 602 sub-brands graded alongside a graded parent, **143 (24%) disagree; 32 by 2+ letters.**
Some disagreements are legitimate (Ben & Jerry's). Many are thin-data artifacts on what is really the
same business (Amazon Go, American Airlines Shuttle, Dollar General Market). Suppress the artifact class.

Also cosmetic but corrosive: **1,744 raw ALL-CAPS EDGAR names** ("CLOROX CO", "J M SMUCKER") are shown to
users; 732 of them are graded and surface in results.

## 3. WHY PEOPLE AREN'T DOWNLOADING OR STAYING

**Acquisition (they never arrive):**
1. **App Store listing is misfiled and keyword-dead.** Per agent research: filed under **News** rather
   than Shopping (the only app in its competitive set that is), named "TruNorthApp" (zero keywords),
   ranks **#4 for its own brand name**, and appears nowhere in the top 12 for any category keyword its
   competitors own. *(Agent-sourced — Aron should confirm in App Store Connect.)*
2. **No distribution mechanism.** Every competitor that grew was carried by a tribe or a creator.
3. **SEO is actively self-harming** while 9,765 pages publish fabricated F's.

**Activation (they arrive and bounce):**
4. **Search fails on the first thing they type** (§2). A user scans/searches Oreo, Tide, Cheerios → nothing.
5. **When it answers, the answer is often "C."** ~45% of consumer brands come back C — nothing to act on.

**Retention (nothing brings them back):**
6. **The "we watch your basket" promise almost never fires** — graded brands change ~0.25%/week.
7. **The one honest, high-volume return trigger — "notify me when we grade this" — is broken**
   (MailerLite 422; plus the weekly rebake notify step at
   `.github/workflows/score-rebake-weekly.yml:105` has been running as a **dry run without `--apply`**).
8. **The paywall taxes evaluation**: 1 full brand profile/day. A new user cannot look up five brands to
   decide the app is worth keeping.

| **Buy'r** | Launched **21 Jan 2026** — 50K downloads week one, #1 Health&Fitness; 4.8★ / **597** ratings | **One creator**: Ian Carroll, 4M+ TikTok followers, promoted directly | $5/mo, $50/yr (3.3× TruNorth) |

| **Good On You** | >1M users, 3,000 brands | Fashion-ethics niche | Freemium |
| **TruNorth** | **5.0★ / 2 ratings**, ~9 visitors/wk | — | $1.99/mo, $14.99/yr |

**Three conclusions:**
1. **Distribution beats product.** A dormant app (Goods Unite Us) has 20,000× TruNorth's review volume.
2. **Demand is proven and current** — Buy'r did 50K downloads in a week, six months ago, on a nearly
   identical loop, with a thinner database and **no scores at all**.
3. **The growth path is one creator, not paid ads.**

**The strategic trade nobody has priced:** every competitor that grew fast is *tribally aligned*
(Boycat=BDS, Buy'r=anti-establishment, Goods Unite Us=progressive, Yuka=clean-eating). TruNorth is
neutral by construction — its 4 stance categories return null and can never move a grade. **That
neutrality is why no movement will carry it for free — and it is also the only positioning that
survives an administration change and that a newsroom or retailer could partner with.** Neutrality is
an asset, but it must be *sold* as one; it will never be free distribution.

**TruNorth's honest wedge:** not "scan everything" (Yuka wins), not "who owns it" (Buy'r wins on
distribution) — but **the neutral, cited, checkable corporate record you reach for after you've heard a
claim.** That wedge requires the correctness work in §1 to be true.

## 5. PRICING — the paywall is inverted

Free today: personalized letter grade, unlimited search, scanner, **1 full brand profile/day.**
Pro gates: the /100 score, all 9 categories, and **the public records behind every grade.**

TruNorth's promise is "records, not opinions" — **and the records are behind the paywall.** A skeptic
arriving from a creator video cannot verify the one thing that differentiates TruNorth without paying.
Meanwhile Yuka gives the entire verdict away and charges $10/yr for convenience.

**Recommendation:** remove the 1-profile/day gate. Make the verdict **and the underlying records** free
and unlimited — that is the marketing. Move Pro to convenience + depth (history, watchlist alerts,
compare, export, category deep-dives) and reposition annual as a **supporter** tier. At ~9 visitors/week
the realistic subscription ceiling is $25–$250/yr; the paywall is currently buying nothing and costing
the word-of-mouth the product needs.

## 8. THE HONEST RISK

The correctness work has **no visible payoff** — no downloads, no traffic, nothing to show anyone. It is
therefore the work most likely to be skipped in favor of the fun distribution work. And then the first
real traffic finds the 23andMe "B", or the Home Depot 2026:1 ratio, or a fabricated F, and the one asset
TruNorth has — being checkable — is gone in a single public fact-check.

**Mitigation:** make it mechanical, not willpower-based (the CI golden set), and hold a written
stop-ship rule: *no distribution work ships while any live page asserts a grade unsupported by a record.*

**Stopping rule worth writing down:** if by **2026-12-15** organic sessions are <100/month and graded
brands with ≥3 categories are still <1,000, stop building and keep the site as a credibility asset.
Not one of 70+ recommendations proposed a stopping rule — that absence is how solo projects consume years.
